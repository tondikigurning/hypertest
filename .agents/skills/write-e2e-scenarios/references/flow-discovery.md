# Discover end-to-end business flows

Use this reference in every exploration mode. The objective is a defensible inventory of the app's in-scope business journeys, including conditional journeys that are easy to miss in a large submodule. Discovery is evidence-based and iterative: product context and source analysis propose candidates, and live `playwright-cli` discovery identifies further candidates and observes what the available environment permits before flow-list confirmation. Neither a source scan nor a single account can prove that every possible journey has been found.

## Decide what is a flow

A flow is a meaningful business journey with a user goal and an observable outcome, possibly spanning screens, roles, services or a sequence of status changes. Trace how the user enters, performs and completes or leaves the journey. Group related interactions and branches under one flow when they serve the same goal; distinguish journeys when the goal, outcome, lifecycle transition or responsible actor is materially different. Do not invent a separate flow for every tab, button, field or technical endpoint. Include shared controls in E2E scenarios when they enable an important business journey.

Prioritize real business outcomes and meaningful lifecycle paths. Include variants when they change the business outcome, permissions, a consequential decision, or an integration boundary; avoid enumerating every validation, visual state, mock error and component behavior merely because the UI exposes it. Use product requirements and user context when available; source code shows what exists but cannot establish business importance or intended behavior. Record assumptions where requirements are absent.

## Build and reconcile the inventory

1. Establish the scope from the route and navigation entries, user context and available requirements. Follow route exports into the actual pages and their reachable imports, including shared feature components, hooks, menus, dialog managers and navigation targets. Inspect loaders/actions and service calls as supporting evidence, not as a list of flows. Trace in-page navigation such as query-parameter workbenches, nested tabs and multi-step dialogs; do not assume every journey has its own route.
2. For each reachable area, trace user goals and outcomes through entry points, action handlers, dialogs, wizards and follow-on navigation. Look for lifecycle transitions, create/update/assignment/closure, related entities, bulk operations, handoffs, and cross-module journeys where applicable. Inspect conditional visibility and enablement (role or permission, record type, status, flags, ownership and external integration). Search the scoped implementation for relevant action labels, handlers, route targets and permission/status checks, then follow their definitions and callers. Adapt searches to the codebase; search results alone are not evidence that a journey is reachable.
3. Reconcile the candidates with requirements, existing feature scenarios and any registry. Preserve existing flow and scenario IDs. An existing tag proves only that some scenarios exist; compare their actual outcomes and business branches with the inventory. Record partially covered journeys and evidence, including existing scenario IDs, rather than assuming a matching title is complete. Group duplicates under the existing flow; keep distinct goals separate. Mark source-only candidates as unverified until observed or otherwise supported; identify dead or unreachable paths only when evidence supports that conclusion.
4. For each candidate record a short goal and outcome, source or requirement evidence, entry point, material preconditions and actors, relevant business branches, existing scenario IDs, priority and verification limitations. In the registry, add concise columns or notes for these facts and for discovery gaps without replacing the existing workflow status. Pending exploration, inaccessible roles, unavailable data, disabled flags, missing specs and uncertain ownership must remain visible rather than silently disappearing. Keep evidence paths specific enough for the next batch to resume.
5. Before asking for flow-list review, cross-check the traced entry points and conditional actions against the inventory. Report the number of candidates, what has been observed, what is only supported by source or requirements, exclusions and unresolved gaps. A user confirming the draft scope does not prove completeness. If the work needs multiple sessions, persist inspected and remaining areas, evidence and blockers in the registry and continue both source and browser discovery in manageable chunks before presenting the list for review. If the browser or environment is unavailable, retain a draft and the blocker, then resume browser discovery when available; do not proceed to scenario authoring from a source-only inventory.

## Explore and revisit

Before flow-list confirmation, explore accessible entry points, menus, tabs, dialogs and possible outcomes across the scope with `playwright-cli`; follow journeys further when safe and feasible. Record UI observations separately from source or product evidence, including inaccessible areas and reasons. This discovery can span sessions and does not itself authorize scenario authoring.

After confirmation, use the live UI to follow the selected journeys from entry to observable business outcome, within data-safety rules. Inspect meaningful alternate paths and conditions where the available role and E2E-owned data allow it. Seed records require explicit scoped approval before mutation, even during discovery; restoration alone is insufficient. If required E2E data cannot be found or provided within scope, explain the limitation and ask permission to use non-E2E data for that specific scenario under [data-safety.md](data-safety.md#exception-review-required-e2e-data-cannot-be-found-or-provisioned). Without approval, skip the mutation. If a role, state, integration or owned data is unavailable, record the exact limitation and source evidence; do not mark the journey absent or verified. Do not turn every discovered UI variation into a scenario.

If browsing or later source analysis reveals a missing or materially different journey, record it in the registry with its evidence and uncertainty, and follow the skill's flow-list confirmation gate before affected work. Preserve prior IDs and review status. At the end of each batch, update the discovery notes and identify what remains unexamined so the next session can continue without relying on memory. Registry workflow status and live verification are separate: `legacy`, `approved`, or `implemented` alone does not establish complete business-flow coverage.
