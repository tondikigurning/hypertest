# Scenario coverage and batching

For a full pass, find and cover all important E2E scenarios supported by the requested scope, active PRD, existing artifacts and live exploration. Smoke is a release-priority label, not the coverage target. Include supported regression scenarios in the same planning pass; reserve smoke-only for an explicit or established scope decision. Do not assume a flow has one scenario or that a happy path covers its alternatives.

## Examine each flow

For each flow selected for the current batch, inspect the following categories against the PRD, shared rules, existing scenarios and observed app behavior. Make an initial lightweight inventory of the wider confirmed scope and record areas needing later investigation; do not delay the first batch until every flow is explored in depth. These are prompts for discovery, not a mandate to invent a scenario in every category:

- Successful outcomes and materially different supported routes, including create/read/update/delete and other main operations.
- Business rules, required inputs, constraints and meaningful boundary or invalid values where they affect an end-to-end outcome; examine relevant individual fields and combinations when their rules differ.
- Distinct user decisions such as confirmation, cancellation and leaving with unsaved changes, when they affect data or business state.
- Relevant roles, access restrictions, ownership and permissions; visible and prohibited actions where evidence permits.
- Lifecycle states, prerequisites, transitions, dependent records and alternative outcomes, including repeat operations when behavior differs.
- Search/filter/sort, empty/no-result, pagination and detail behavior when necessary to find, understand or act on business records.
- Failure and recovery at meaningful service, authentication or integration boundaries, including retry, when supported by requirements or evidence and the applicable mock policy.
- Known defects, explicitly requested behaviors, cross-menu journeys and integration effects.

For each applicable branch, identify its source or observation, preconditions and observable outcome. Compare these with existing scenario assertions, not just titles or flow tags. Before setting the batch boundary, make a concise per-flow inventory of identified branches: existing coverage, candidates to author now, and branches needing evidence or blocked, with a reason and next action for each unresolved branch. Identify categories not yet examined without inventing branches; investigate feasible gaps as the batch progresses. Author distinct scenarios when conditions or outcomes exercise materially different business behavior; reuse an existing scenario or shared contract when it actually covers the branch. Do not multiply equivalent input samples, incidental UI states or component-only behavior without a separate E2E reason. Do not invent behavior, data, access or mock contracts; record an unverified or blocked branch and its reason instead. One scenario is valid only if the examined flow has no other supported, important uncovered branch; explain the result or gaps in review.

Classify each applicable branch as already covered, to be written in this batch, deferred to a later batch, blocked/unverified, or excluded by the user's established scope. Report why and the next action for every unresolved branch in the scenario handoff and review output. Keep the flow-registry table unchanged; use its Gaps section only for PRD/actual mismatches. A flow remains `pending` until all its confirmed in-scope scenarios are written. Do not claim full coverage from the number of flows or scenarios, or from unavailable roles and data.

For example, if a create flow's sources and exploration establish successful creation, duplicate-name rejection, cancellation without creating a record and a role denied access, assess all four outcomes separately. A success assertion alone covers only the first. If the denied role is unavailable, keep that branch unverified and identify the missing access; do not invent its UI outcome. If duplicate rejection and cancellation are accessible and not already asserted, include them as regression candidates. This example does not establish behavior in another menu.

## Choose a batch

For both terminal agents and Q-Rex, inventory and confirm discovered flows across the requested scope before planning scenario authoring. Register every confirmed flow, including those assigned to later batches, and record individual unconfirmed candidates in `.exploration.md`. Plan to examine and author the entire confirmed scope, including flows in different feature files; carry this scope forward when the workflow resumes.

When there are 12 or fewer confirmed flows, process the entire scope together without splitting it into batches. For 13 or more, partition the entire inventory into balanced batches targeting 8–12 flows each. Use the fewest batches needed to keep each at or below 12 and distribute flows as evenly as possible: for example, 13 becomes 7 and 6, 20 becomes 10 and 10, and 25 becomes 9, 8 and 8. A smaller batch is acceptable when the total cannot be divided into groups of at least 8 or when a concrete evidence, host or review constraint requires it. Group related flows where practical, retain exact feature paths, and record the ordered plan and outstanding flows in `.exploration.md`. Reconcile the plan if discovery or confirmation changes the inventory.

Assess every flow in the current batch independently. Enumerate candidates from the PRD, shared rules, existing scenarios and reusable exploration evidence. Begin with the primary successful outcome, then examine all applicable checklist categories and author every distinct supported E2E outcome and branch, including regression. There is no per-flow or per-batch scenario-count target or cap; the number of flows in a batch must not reduce coverage within a flow. Count existing scenarios only when their assertions cover the outcome and avoid equivalent duplicates. Account individually for unsupported, blocked or unverified branches.

Investigate feasible, authorized read-only evidence gaps for each current flow before its review, such as the effects of visible filters, sorting, tabs or detail drawers. Prioritize business risk and main lifecycle outcomes when ordering work. Different prerequisites or future implementation effort alone do not exclude a flow. A blocked mutation, unavailable role or missing integration evidence limits only affected branches: record the blocker and next action, and continue independent work within data-safety scope. If a concrete constraint requires splitting one flow across batches, preserve its ID, record its remaining branches and revisit them when the constraint is resolved.

Before presenting each batch, reconcile each current-flow candidate as existing coverage, newly authored, equivalent, unsupported or blocked/unverified with its reason and next action. Report the exact flow/feature/scenario scope, remaining branches and later batches in the scenario handoff and review. Record PRD/actual mismatches in registry Gaps. Continue through the planned scenario-only batches under the shared contract: in manual mode stop for each required review and resume after its decision; in explicitly enabled Q-Rex automatic mode continue through the automatic gates. Account for Q-Rex's cumulative review snapshot, and report implementation and verification separately.
